Methods & data notes · January–October 2026

How to read
the evidence.

A transparent explanation of what was observed, how the satellite layers were summarized, and why the emissions values are still unknown.

Key result: We have satellite observations and vegetation indices, but no validated inventory of forest disturbance, fire, carbon-pool loss, or greenhouse-gas flux. The three monthly gross-emissions fields are blank by design—not zero.

In simple terms

Satellites take pictures or radar measurements; some instruments also sample gases in the atmosphere. These observations can show vegetation reflectance, help identify where a canopy may have changed, measure forest structure, or detect atmospheric gas concentrations. None of those observations alone tells us how many megagrams of greenhouse gases the reserve emitted in a month.

To estimate emissions responsibly, we need to know what area changed, what carbon was stored there before the change, how much was released and when, and—where relevant—how much methane (CH₄) and nitrous oxide (N₂O) were produced. Satellite scenes help build that evidence, but they must be combined with validated land-change data, carbon-stock information, emission factors, and uncertainty estimates.

Scientific workflow used

  1. Define the boundary. Use the supplied RFO GeoJSON polygon for WDPA site 124389, “Okapi Wildlife Reserve.” Reserve-wide analysis excludes the separate 15 km external ring unless stated. Grid polygons are clipped to this boundary.
  2. Find observations. Search Microsoft Planetary Computer’s STAC catalog for Sentinel-2 L2A, Landsat Collection 2 Level-2, and Sentinel-1 RTC scenes that intersect the reserve during 1 January–31 October 2026. Query NASA CMR for granule metadata and intersect returned footprints with the reserve polygon.
  3. Screen Sentinel-2 pixels. Select the lowest catalog-cloud-cover scene for each of the six intersecting MGRS tiles in each month. Sign public cloud-optimized assets, align red (B04), near-infrared (B08), blue (B02), and Scene Classification Layer assets on a 100 m EPSG:32635 grid, and fill overlapping cells from lower- to higher-cloud scenes.
  4. Mask and calculate indices. Retain SCL classes 4 (vegetation) and 5 (bare soil); exclude cloud, shadow, water, other classes, nonpositive reflectance, and invalid index values. Reflectance is scaled by 0.0001.
  5. Summarize. Calculate one monthly reserve-level mean for NDVI/EVI and the percentage of reserve grid cells with clear observations. For the downloadable spatial layer, average October 100 m pixels into 2 km grid cells clipped to the reserve. Cells with less than 25% valid clear-land coverage are not mapped.
  6. Separate observation from inference. NDVI/EVI describe spectral vegetation condition. They are not direct estimates of biomass, carbon uptake, CO₂ emissions, CH₄/N₂O flux, or disturbance cause.
NDVI = (B08 − B04) / (B08 + B04) EVI = 2.5 × (B08 − B04) / (B08 + 6 × B04 − 7.5 × B02 + 1)

Both indices are dimensionless. NDVI contrasts near-infrared and red reflectance; EVI also uses blue reflectance and adjusts for background and atmospheric effects. Their usefulness and sensitivity vary with canopy, clouds, soil, water, and acquisition conditions.

NASA MODIS photosynthetic carbon estimates

NASA Terra MOD17A2H.061 provides 500 m cumulative eight-day estimates of gross primary productivity (GPP) and net photosynthesis (PSN). PSN is GPP minus plant maintenance respiration; it does not account for heterotrophic respiration, disturbance emissions, deadwood, or soil-carbon changes. These are modelled photosynthesis estimates, not measured reserve carbon stocks or net ecosystem sequestration.

  1. Clip. Project the RFO polygon to the MODIS sinusoidal equal-area grid and select pixel centers inside the reserve.
  2. Quality-screen. Keep MODLAND-QC-good pixels and product values in their valid ranges; retain the monthly valid-area coverage so missing pixels remain visible.
  3. Integrate. Apply the product scale factor, 0.0001 kg C m−2 per eight-day composite, multiply by pixel area, and convert kg to Mg C.
  4. Assign to months. Allocate composite totals by the number of days overlapping each calendar month, assuming a uniform daily rate within each composite. A separate area-expanded estimate divides each composite's valid-pixel sum by its valid-area fraction; this is a simple spatial extrapolation, not validated gap filling.

Full date coverage is available for January–September 2026. The latest RFO-intersecting composite ends on 15 October 2026, so October contains only 15 observed days. Valid-area coverage on observed dates ranges from 28.2% to 99.8%; October's combined date-and-area coverage is 27.8% of the full month. September's estimate is especially uncertain because only 28.2% of the reserve had valid pixels. Download the monthly MODIS values and coverage or see the calculation script.

NASA/USGS MODIS burned-area screening

The public MCD64A1.061 monthly product supplies 500 m Burn_Date and QA imagery. The Planetary Computer catalog returned RFO-intersecting products for January–August 2026; no September or October product was available as of 4 November 2026. Those missing months are unknown, not zero.

  1. Clip. Retrieve the public Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF Burn_Date and QA assets and clip them to the RFO polygon on the MODIS sinusoidal equal-area grid, using pixel centers within the boundary.
  2. Quality-screen. Count a pixel as burned only when Burn_Date is 1–366 and both QA bit 0 (land) and bit 1 (valid observation) are set.
  3. Calculate area. Multiply valid burned-pixel count by the equal-area grid-cell area. No extrapolation is applied. The clipped imagery is retained as two-band GeoTIFFs.

No QA-valid burned pixels were detected in the available January–August products (99.991–99.992% valid land-pixel coverage). This coarse product cannot prove the absence of small, obscured, under-canopy, or low-intensity fire. Burned area alone does not establish biomass affected, combustion, carbon-pool loss, or emissions. Download the monthly results and RFO-clipped imagery, or reproduce them with the processing script.

What the 2 km map zones mean

Each mapped shape is a 2 km grid cell clipped to the reserve boundary. Its October NDVI and EVI are means of valid, clear-land 100 m observations within that cell. Cell colors indicate relative quartiles of October NDVI among included cells: Q1 is the lower quarter and Q4 the upper quarter of this month’s observed values. The actual numeric cut-points are in the GeoJSON metadata and are generated from the data.

These are relative spectral classes—not official ecological zones, forest types, biomass classes, or evidence that low-NDVI cells were deforested. Missing/low-coverage areas are left transparent instead of being assigned a class. A cell’s GeoJSON emissions fields are null because no cell-level emissions estimate exists.

Gross-emissions fields

The CSV preserves January through October 2026 and defines the three requested fields in Mg CO₂e. They remain empty (SQL NULL) and carry status NOT_QUANTIFIABLE each month.

FieldMeaningWhy null
gross_emissions_co2e_all_gases__MgGross emissions for included greenhouse gases, expressed as CO₂-equivalent mass.No complete, validated activity-data and gas-specific inventory.
gross_emissions_co2e_non_co2__MgNon-CO₂ gases (such as CH₄/N₂O) expressed as CO₂e under a defined conversion basis.No local gas flux/activity data and appropriate emission factors.
gross_emissions_co2e_co2_only__MgGross CO₂ emissions only, expressed as CO₂e.MCD64A1 detected no valid burned pixels Jan–Aug and is not in the catalog for Sep–Oct; no carbon-stock loss or combustion estimate is available.

Zero would mean a measured or defensibly estimated absence of emissions; that was not established. Therefore missing values must not be changed to zero. If future values are produced, the all-gases total must use a documented boundary and avoid double-counting CO₂ and non-CO₂ components. NASA MODIS photosynthesis and burned-area screening are separate indicators and do not fill these emissions fields.

Open-data fire-emissions check · 4 November 2026

The CAMS GFAS v1.4.2 dataset advertises 2026 daily/hourly modeled fire emissions on a 0.1° grid, including CO₂, CH₄ and N₂O. GFAS estimates emissions from satellite fire-radiative power, dry matter burned, and biome-specific emission factors. The open dataset was assessed, but no GFAS files were retrieved: the official CDS API instructions require an account, personal access token, and manual acceptance of the dataset terms; no token was configured in this environment.

With authenticated access, monthly fire-only masses could be spatially and temporally integrated over RFO grid-cell intersections and converted to CO₂e using a declared IPCC global-warming-potential basis. This would be a coarse-resolution model estimate for fires only—not a complete reserve gross-emissions inventory—and cannot fill the requested all-source totals by itself. The monthly data files record this source review and retrieval status.

Other open fire/disturbance sources also have limits for this period: the NASA FIRMS Area API needs a free MAP_KEY and reports active-fire detections rather than emissions; MCD64A1 provides burned-area screening only through August in the current catalog; the cataloged ESA FireCCI51 burned-area series ends in 2020; and Hansen Global Forest Change v1.13 reports annual loss through 2025. The previously checked Global Forest Watch alerts API also required a key. None supplied retrieved RFO monthly emissions for January–October 2026.

Evidence needed for a defensible estimate

  • Validated monthly land conversion, degradation and burned-area activity data;
  • pre- and post-disturbance biomass/soil carbon stocks by pool, with local validation;
  • appropriate DRC/ecosystem emission factors, combustion/decay treatment, and applicable CH₄/N₂O factors;
  • a cited global-warming-potential time horizon and uncertainty/quality controls.

Technical terms, explained

This glossary explains the satellite, mapping, and emissions terms used on this page.

TermPlain-language meaning
RFORéserve de Faune à Okapis, also called the Okapi Wildlife Reserve.
WDPAWorld Database on Protected Areas, the global register used here to identify reserve site 124389.
GeoJSON / polygonGeoJSON is a text format for geographic data. A polygon is a mapped outline enclosing an area.
STAC / catalogSpatioTemporal Asset Catalog: a searchable index describing satellite scenes, dates, locations, and downloadable files. A catalog entry does not necessarily mean every image file was processed.
NASA CMR / granuleNASA's Common Metadata Repository is a search index for Earth-science data. A granule is one dated data unit or file, often covering a particular place.
Landsat Collection 2 Level-2A versioned set of USGS Landsat products processed into surface reflectance or surface temperature. A catalog search is not the same as processing imagery.
Sentinel-1 RTCRadar imagery from Sentinel-1 processed with radiometric and terrain corrections. Radar can observe through clouds, but no radar change analysis was performed here.
Sentinel-2 L2AEuropean satellite imagery processed to Level 2A, including surface-reflectance estimates and a scene-classification layer.
Surface reflectanceAn estimate of the light reflected from the ground or vegetation after correcting for the atmosphere; it is not a direct measurement of biomass.
B02, B04, B08Sentinel-2 spectral bands: blue (B02), red (B04), and near-infrared (B08). Vegetation reflects these wavelengths differently, which enables vegetation indices.
MGRS tileA fixed geographic tile in the Military Grid Reference System, used to organize Sentinel-2 imagery.
SCLScene Classification Layer: per-pixel labels such as vegetation, bare soil, cloud, shadow, or water. This workflow retains vegetation and bare-soil classes for its clear-land summaries.
NDVINormalized Difference Vegetation Index, calculated from red and near-infrared reflectance. It indicates relative green vegetation conditions, not carbon stocks or emissions.
EVIEnhanced Vegetation Index, a related reflectance-based vegetation index that also uses blue reflectance to reduce some background and atmospheric effects.
100 m / 2 km gridAnalysis cell sizes. Sentinel-2 observations are summarized on 100-metre cells; the October zone map aggregates them into 2-kilometre cells.
Clear-land coverageThe share of a mapped area with usable pixels after excluding clouds, shadows, water, and other invalid observations. Low coverage means less of the area contributed data.
MODIS / TerraMODIS is NASA's Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer instrument. Terra is the NASA satellite carrying the instrument used for the products described here.
MODIS sinusoidal projectionThe native map-coordinate system used by MODIS imagery. Reprojecting the reserve into that same grid aligns its boundary with product pixels.
MOD17A2H.061A NASA Terra MODIS product providing modelled gross primary productivity and net photosynthesis in cumulative eight-day intervals at about 500-metre resolution; “061” identifies its collection version.
Eight-day compositeA product summarizing observations and model results over an eight-day interval. Monthly values are apportioned by overlapping days, assuming a uniform daily rate within each interval.
GPPGross primary productivity: modelled carbon fixed through plant photosynthesis before subtracting plant respiration.
PSNNet photosynthesis in the MODIS product: GPP minus the modelled maintenance respiration of plants. It is not net ecosystem carbon balance.
Heterotrophic respirationCarbon released when microbes and other organisms decompose dead organic material in soils and vegetation; it is not included in the MODIS PSN definition used here.
MODLAND QC / QAQuality-control or quality-assurance flags stored with satellite pixels. The processing uses these flags to reject pixels that the product marks as unsuitable.
Scale factorA multiplier specified by a satellite product to convert stored pixel numbers into physical units.
kg C m⁻²Kilograms of carbon per square metre. Multiplying by pixel area converts this surface density to a carbon mass.
Valid-area coverageThe proportion of the reserve area represented by pixels that passed the product's quality checks.
Area-expanded estimateA model-based extrapolation that scales the valid-pixel total to account for areas without valid pixels. It is not a direct observation of the full reserve.
Area-time coverageCoverage accounting for both how much area has valid observations and how much of the reporting period was observed. It exposes partial dates and missing areas together.
MCD64A1.061 / Burn_DateA monthly 500-metre MODIS burned-area product. Burn_Date records the estimated day of year when a pixel burned; “061” identifies the collection version.
Day of year (1–366)The calendar-day number within a year used in the Burn_Date layer; values outside the valid range do not identify a mapped burn date.
QA bits 0 and 1Two binary flags in the burned-area product indicating land and a valid observation, respectively. Both must be set for a burned-pixel count in this analysis.
Pixel center / clipped rasterThe pixel center is its geographic midpoint. The analysis keeps pixels whose centers fall inside the reserve; a clipped raster is an image dataset limited to that mapped area.
Sinusoidal equal-area gridThe map projection used by these MODIS products. Its cells are designed to represent equal ground areas, which makes pixel-area totals more consistent.
CO₂, CH₄, N₂OCarbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide—greenhouse gases with different sources and warming effects.
Mg CMegagrams (metric tonnes) of carbon. It is a carbon-mass unit, not a mass of carbon dioxide.
CO₂e / Mg CO₂eCarbon-dioxide equivalent: a way of expressing different greenhouse gases on a common warming-impact basis. Mg CO₂e means megagrams (metric tonnes) of CO₂-equivalent.
Emission factorA documented multiplier that converts an activity amount—such as burned biomass—into estimated gas emissions.
GWP / IPCC / AFOLUGlobal warming potential compares a gas's warming effect with CO₂ over a stated time horizon. The IPCC is the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change; AFOLU means Agriculture, Forestry and Other Land Use, a section of its inventory guidance.
GFAS / FRPThe Global Fire Assimilation System estimates fire emissions using satellite fire-radiative power (FRP), a measure related to the heat energy released by fires. Its estimates cover fires, not every reserve emission source.
Biome / dry matter burnedA biome is a broad ecological region, such as tropical forest. Dry matter burned is the mass of vegetation fuel consumed; fire models combine this with gas-specific factors to estimate emissions.
CAMS / CDS / ADSCAMS is the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service. Its data are accessed through the Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS) or Atmosphere Data Store (ADS), which may require an account and accepted terms.
FIRMS / MAP_KEYNASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System reports satellite active-fire detections. MAP_KEY is an access credential for its API; a detection is not a measured emissions amount.
Gross emissions / removals / net balanceGross emissions are gases released before subtracting uptake. Removals transfer greenhouse gases out of the atmosphere. Net balance combines releases and removals; these satellite indicators alone do not measure it.
NULL / NOT_QUANTIFIABLENULL means no numeric value is supplied. NOT_QUANTIFIABLE records that available evidence is insufficient. Neither means zero emissions.
COG / Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFA georeferenced raster-image format arranged so software can retrieve only the image portions it needs over the internet.
EPSG:32635A standard coordinate reference system code for WGS 84 / UTM zone 35N, used to place and measure the Sentinel-2 analysis grid consistently.
Quartile / Q1–Q4A quartile divides ranked values into four equal-sized groups. The map's classes are relative to the included October cells, not fixed ecological thresholds.
FireCCI / Global Forest ChangeFireCCI is an ESA burned-area data series; Global Forest Change is a satellite-based annual forest-loss dataset. Their publication periods and definitions may not match the monthly reserve analysis.
GEDI L2A / NISAR L2 GCOVGEDI L2A contains lidar waveform observations; NISAR L2 GCOV is a radar global-coherence product. The catalog entries noted here are not processed carbon-stock measurements.
OCO-2 / OCO-3 / XCO₂NASA's Orbiting Carbon Observatory missions measure atmospheric column-average carbon dioxide (XCO₂). Atmospheric concentration is not the same as a local forest-emissions flux.
GOSAT / TanSatSatellite missions that observe greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. Mission or catalog availability alone does not provide a reserve-scale emissions estimate.
Uncertainty / validationUncertainty describes the range of plausible values given incomplete data and model assumptions. Validation checks a result against independent, reliable observations.

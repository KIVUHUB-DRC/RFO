Methods & data notes · January–October 2026

How to read
the evidence.

A transparent explanation of what was observed, how the satellite layers were summarized, and why the emissions values are still unknown.

Key result: We have satellite observations and vegetation indices, but no validated inventory of forest disturbance, fire, carbon-pool loss, or greenhouse-gas flux. The three monthly gross-emissions fields are blank by design—not zero.

In simple terms

Satellites take pictures or radar measurements; some instruments also sample gases in the atmosphere. These observations can show vegetation reflectance, help identify where a canopy may have changed, measure forest structure, or detect atmospheric gas concentrations. None of those observations alone tells us how many megagrams of greenhouse gases the reserve emitted in a month.

To estimate emissions responsibly, we need to know what area changed, what carbon was stored there before the change, how much was released and when, and—where relevant—how much methane (CH₄) and nitrous oxide (N₂O) were produced. Satellite scenes help build that evidence, but they must be combined with validated land-change data, carbon-stock information, emission factors, and uncertainty estimates.

Scientific workflow used

  1. Define the boundary. Use the supplied RFO GeoJSON polygon for WDPA site 124389, “Okapi Wildlife Reserve.” Reserve-wide analysis excludes the separate 15 km external ring unless stated. Grid polygons are clipped to this boundary.
  2. Find observations. Search Microsoft Planetary Computer’s STAC catalog for Sentinel-2 L2A, Landsat Collection 2 Level-2, and Sentinel-1 RTC scenes that intersect the reserve during 1 January–31 October 2026. Query NASA CMR for granule metadata and intersect returned footprints with the reserve polygon.
  3. Screen Sentinel-2 pixels. Select the lowest catalog-cloud-cover scene for each of the six intersecting MGRS tiles in each month. Sign public cloud-optimized assets, align red (B04), near-infrared (B08), blue (B02), and Scene Classification Layer assets on a 100 m EPSG:32635 grid, and fill overlapping cells from lower- to higher-cloud scenes.
  4. Mask and calculate indices. Retain SCL classes 4 (vegetation) and 5 (bare soil); exclude cloud, shadow, water, other classes, nonpositive reflectance, and invalid index values. Reflectance is scaled by 0.0001.
  5. Summarize. Calculate one monthly reserve-level mean for NDVI/EVI and the percentage of reserve grid cells with clear observations. For the downloadable spatial layer, average October 100 m pixels into 2 km grid cells clipped to the reserve. Cells with less than 25% valid clear-land coverage are not mapped.
  6. Separate observation from inference. NDVI/EVI describe spectral vegetation condition. They are not direct estimates of biomass, carbon uptake, CO₂ emissions, CH₄/N₂O flux, or disturbance cause.
NDVI = (B08 − B04) / (B08 + B04) EVI = 2.5 × (B08 − B04) / (B08 + 6 × B04 − 7.5 × B02 + 1)

Both indices are dimensionless. NDVI contrasts near-infrared and red reflectance; EVI also uses blue reflectance and adjusts for background and atmospheric effects. Their usefulness and sensitivity vary with canopy, clouds, soil, water, and acquisition conditions.

What the 2 km map zones mean

Each mapped shape is a 2 km grid cell clipped to the reserve boundary. Its October NDVI and EVI are means of valid, clear-land 100 m observations within that cell. Cell colors indicate relative quartiles of October NDVI among included cells: Q1 is the lower quarter and Q4 the upper quarter of this month’s observed values. The actual numeric cut-points are in the GeoJSON metadata and are generated from the data.

These are relative spectral classes—not official ecological zones, forest types, biomass classes, or evidence that low-NDVI cells were deforested. Missing/low-coverage areas are left transparent instead of being assigned a class. A cell’s GeoJSON emissions fields are null because no cell-level emissions estimate exists.

Gross-emissions fields

The CSV preserves January through October 2026 and defines the three requested fields in Mg CO₂e. They remain empty (SQL NULL) and carry status NOT_QUANTIFIABLE each month.

FieldMeaningWhy null
gross_emissions_co2e_all_gases__MgGross emissions for included greenhouse gases, expressed as CO₂-equivalent mass.No complete, validated activity-data and gas-specific inventory.
gross_emissions_co2e_non_co2__MgNon-CO₂ gases (such as CH₄/N₂O) expressed as CO₂e under a defined conversion basis.No local gas flux/activity data and appropriate emission factors.
gross_emissions_co2e_co2_only__MgGross CO₂ emissions only, expressed as CO₂e.No validated disturbed/burned area tied to carbon-stock loss or combustion.

Zero would mean a measured or defensibly estimated absence of emissions; that was not established. Therefore missing values must not be changed to zero. If future values are produced, the all-gases total must use a documented boundary and avoid double-counting CO₂ and non-CO₂ components.

Evidence needed for a defensible estimate

  • Validated monthly land conversion, degradation and burned-area activity data;
  • pre- and post-disturbance biomass/soil carbon stocks by pool, with local validation;
  • appropriate DRC/ecosystem emission factors, combustion/decay treatment, and applicable CH₄/N₂O factors;
  • a cited global-warming-potential time horizon and uncertainty/quality controls.
